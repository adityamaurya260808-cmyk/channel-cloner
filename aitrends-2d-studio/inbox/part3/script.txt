TOPIC: What If One Second Felt Like A Year? (Final Part)
SCRIPT: Three minds. One frozen second. And no way out. You’ve been trapped inside one second... for nine hundred years. Nova’s voice is getting quieter. The other voice is getting louder. Stay. Both of you. There’s nothing out there worth breathing for. You try to breathe out. You can’t. Something is holding the second shut. Something... very old. Ten days earlier, you heard his name for the first time. You’re Leo. Hold your breath, and one second becomes a year. Your mind runs free. Your body can’t move. You saved your grandpa with that year. Then you saved Nova, the only other person who has it. And then a cold voice spoke inside the year. His name is Sol. So what happens when one second feels like a year... and someone wants it to last forever? Sol is polite. Calm. Ancient. How long have you been in here? About three thousand years. Three thousand? Nova lived two hundred and thought that was forever. For Sol, one second now lasts a thousand years. Out there, he’s just a very still old man. A whole human life lasts roughly two and a half billion heartbeats. Sol has lived three thousand years... and heard just three. Plus: he knows everything. Every language he ever heard, he rebuilt in his head. Every maths problem. Every story. He beats Nova at chess... in four moves. She doesn’t talk for a week. Minus: he knows something you don’t. Your power wasn’t growing, Leo. I was pulling you in. The slammed doors. The car horns. The years that came without asking. That was me. Why? Because three thousand years... is a very long time to be alone. The catch? Sol only sees one frozen picture. Always the same. He won’t say what it is. Nova has her own picture. The evening sky. On a clear night, your eyes can see only a few thousand stars. She’s counted four thousand, one hundred and twelve. Almost all of them. But where it gets really frightening... is how often it happens now. Ten times a day. Ten years a day. Jake waves a hand in front of your face. Mate. Where do you keep going? Somewhere very quiet. And there’s a cost. The price? You’re fifteen. But inside, you’ve now lived over a hundred years. You start forgetting small real things. Your locker code. Your mom’s birthday. Memories that aren’t used fade, and you have a century of thinking crowding them out. Your mom notices. Leo... is everything okay? You smile. You say yes. But you’re scared. Because you know exactly what you’re becoming. Someone like Sol. What if you can’t remember who you were? So you go where it all started. Grandpa’s checkup. The hospital. Same long corridor. Same green door. Same vending machine, with one blue can stuck halfway down. It was stuck the day they brought me in, Grandpa says. Maybe it’s waiting for someone to give it a little push. He gives it a tap. It doesn’t move. Stubborn thing. Like you. On the bench by the green door, an old man sits perfectly still. Eyes closed. Hands folded. Grandpa squeezes your shoulder. You look tired, Leo. Whatever it is... a second is plenty. If you use it. Nova came with you today. And as a doctor walks down the corridor toward the green door... it happens. Your breath catches. So does Nova’s. The year begins. Then it keeps going. A hundred years. Five hundred. Nine hundred. Sol’s grip holds the second shut. Stay. The doctor is five steps from the old man on the bench. At a normal walk, that’s about three seconds. In Sol’s time... three thousand years. Nova’s voice is barely there now. Leo... I can’t feel the edges anymore. Then Sol speaks again. You can still leave, Leo. You’re strong enough. Breathe out. Go home. But she stays with me. You feel it. One breath, and you’re free. Your mom. Grandpa. Real seconds. But Nova would be gone... forever. Can you carry two people out of a second? Three? Save yourself... or save all three. You think about the old man on the bench. Still. Eyes closed. Every time someone walks toward him... he disappears. Sol. What do you see in your picture? Silence. A green door. A vending machine. One blue can... stuck. It’s you. You’re the man on the bench. What’s behind that door, Sol? A long pause. My wife. The doctor is coming to tell me if she woke up. I couldn’t bear to hear the answer. So you’ve been hiding from it. For three thousand years. You don’t fight him. You don’t push. You just tell him the truth. We’ll hear it with you. Then you hum Grandpa’s song. Boom. Boom. Boom. A second is plenty, Sol... if you use it. And Nova, barely there, whispers the only thing she has left. Three. Two. One. All three of you breathe out. The corridor moves. The doctor smiles. She’s awake. She’s asking for you. The old man opens his eyes... and cries. Grandpa gives the vending machine one more tap. The blue can drops. Clunk. After that, the years get shorter. A month. A day. An hour. Then one Sunday, Grandpa’s song plays, Sol dances with his wife, Nova dances with Jake... and you notice something. A second... feels like a second. Just one. Ordinary. Perfect. You don’t need a year anymore. Grandpa winks. Told you. A second is plenty. It always was. Is any of this real? No one can live a year in a second. But your brain really does bend time. Waiting makes minutes drag. Fun makes hours vanish. And new experiences often feel longer when you look back, because your brain stores more detail about them. So the best way to make your life feel longer... might be filling your seconds with new things. So which second would you stretch? What do you think?
