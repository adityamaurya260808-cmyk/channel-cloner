A wasp drifts toward your sandwich. You watch it come. Slowly. Like a feather sinking through honey. You have time to count its stripes... and step aside. Your mom drops a glass. You see it tip. You see it fall. You remember where the dustpan is... before it even reaches the floor. Your little brother blinks. To you, that blink takes ten seconds. Theo, why are you staring at me? Because, Sam... your eyelid is still closing. And your toast? It still hasn’t popped. You’ve been waiting for it for what feels like an hour. Your name is Theo. You’re fourteen. A normal person reacts to something they see in about a quarter of a second. You? Two and a half thousandths. A hundred times faster. Drop a ruler between most people’s fingers, and it falls about thirty centimeters before they grab it. Drop one for you... and your brain notices after three hundredths of a millimeter. The catch? Your brain got a hundred times faster. Your body did not. Your nerves still carry signals at about a hundred meters per second. Your legs still run like any fourteen-year-old’s. You see everything coming... you just can’t move any faster to meet it. So what if your reflexes were a hundred times faster? Plus: you never lose at catch. Your best friend Mia throws a ball at your face. You have time to yawn. Then you catch it. How... how did you even see that? I see everything, Mia. Okay. That’s actually creepy. Video games? You read every enemy before it moves. Forty wins in a row. Then the game bans you for cheating. Dude. Even the computer thinks you’re cheating. Movies? A film shows twenty-four pictures every second. Everyone else sees motion. You see... a slideshow. Click. Click. Click. Great movie, right? Very... clicky. Minus: some old lights flicker a hundred times a second. Nobody notices. You do. Your school hallway strobes like a nightclub. Then there’s track. Coach Lee fires the starting gun. You’re gone before the smoke clears. And disqualified. Leave the blocks less than a tenth of a second after the gun, and the rules decide you must have guessed. The best sprinters react in about fifteen hundredths. Nobody beats a tenth. Nobody... except you. So you learn the strangest skill in sport. Waiting. Coach Lee hands you a rule. Count, Theo. Count to what? Count until you’re on time. Fast doesn’t matter. On time does.
