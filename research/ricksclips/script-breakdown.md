- **Biggest hits (14M to 32M):** a story with a character the crowd laughs at or doubts, then a **real-world mechanism** (physics, law, invention) explains the payoff. Railroad gaps, storm oil, the earthquake jar, Japan's "nothing room", the pachinko token window.

## 2. The script formula

Nearly every video follows the same five beats.

### Beat 1: The "wrong-sounding" cold open (0 to 3s)
The first sentence states the **strangest, most out-of-context action** in the story, with no setup. Usually 6 to 20 words, past or present tense, about a "he" or "they".

Patterns used:
- **"He [did a baffling thing]."** "He saw gaps into a railroad every 39 ft." / "He poured cooking oil into the sea…" / "He bet his life savings on the slowest horse in the race."
- **"They [did a baffling thing]."** "They painted the jail cell pink." / "They buried a man alive with a bell tied to his finger."
- **"[X] is illegal/impossible, yet [contradiction]."** "Gambling is illegal in Japan. Yet, there's a gambling hall on almost every street."
- **Second person "you".** "If your ship got hit by a cannonball, you'd actually be one of the lucky ones."
- **Direct question.** "How do painkillers know where the pain is?" (Weakest performer. Questions underperform statements here.)

The opener is often the **ending, told first**. The viewer sees the outcome and has to stay to learn why.

### Beat 2: "You see," plus fast context (3 to 10s)
The phrase **"You see,"** appears in 15 of 20 scripts. It's the channel's verbal signature. It's used twice:
1. Right after the hook, to drop in the world or stakes ("You see, two companies are racing to build a railroad…").
2. Again at the reveal, to switch from story to explanation ("You see, sailors really carried oil just for this.").

Context is given in one or two sentences max: who is competing, what's at stake, what the rule of the world is.

### Beat 3: Ridicule and doubt (10 to 35s)
Someone **laughs at, mocks or doubts** the main character. This shows up in almost every hit:
- The boss's face "goes purple" over the rail gaps.
- The captain asks if he's "making soup" while pirates close in.
- The betting room calls him "mirror man".
- The court laughs at the jar inventor, then the guards laugh at him a second time.
- The cop "laughs all the way back to his car."

Mockers get **short, punchy, funny lines of dialogue**, often a one-liner joke ("Barbie's going to love it in here"; the horse that "lost a race to the milk cart"). That builds the payoff: the audience wants the doubters proven wrong.

Pacing tools here:
- **Escalation with numbers and times:** "In May… By July…", "At 15 minutes… At 20… by the time…", "5 minutes go by, then 10…".
- **A rule of three:** three gun duels, three time checks, "Then another. Then all eight at once."
- **Fake-out:** a small event where "nothing happens" before the big one (the first ball falls and nothing happens; the button press does nothing at first).

### Beat 4: Mid-video re-hook (about 20 to 30s)
A **"Wait, what?"** beat or a viewer-voice question resets attention halfway through:
- "Wait, what?" (pink cell)
- "Wait, what's a mirror going to do?" (horse race)
- "Wait, practiced?" (grave bell)
- "Does he know what he's doing? Not really." (roller coaster)
- "How is that possible?" (gunfight)

The narrator **asks the question the viewer is thinking**, then answers it with "Well, …".

### Beat 5: Climax, then "You see" reveal, then a last-line sting (last 15 to 20s)
1. **Climax image:** the big moment is shown just before it's explained (the wave rises; the rival's cart reaches the loop; the train rolls onto the track).
2. **"You see," reveal:** the real mechanism in one or two sentences.
3. **An everyday analogy:** the science is always tied to something you've done yourself:
   - Blowing on soup → why oil stops waves.
   - Catching your phone as it falls → reflexes beat decisions.
   - Taking a hairpin turn at 5 vs 60 → loop forces.
   - Smoke alarms in every room → painkillers.
4. **Final line = the twist or callback,** often cut off mid-sentence so the video loops (railroad and storm oil both end mid-sentence: "…right as a train rolls onto" / "…drops straight onto the pirates'"). The cut-off ending makes the loop back to the hook feel seamless and pushes rewatches.

## 3. Voice and writing style rules

- **Present tense for the story** ("he grabs a hammer", "the captain turns the wheel") even when the hook was past tense. That makes it feel live.
- **Sentence length:** about 14 words on average, mixed with very short punches ("But he won't close a single one." / "So, he waits.").
- **Connective words that keep momentum:** "So,", "But", "Then", "Until", "Meanwhile", "Instead". Almost every sentence starts with one. There are no paragraph-style pauses.
- **Casual comedy:** modern slang in old settings ("Bro, it's just warming up", "Pal, that horse…"), self-aware jokes ("So, he does what any sane person would do. He blows his entire severance check…").
- **Specific numbers make it believable:** 39 ft, one pint into a gallon, 15 minutes, 300 lb, 47 scratches, 90%, 50 yards.
- **Concrete sensory verbs:** "crushed like a soda can", "snap like a twig", "kicks sideways like a snake", "lit up from the inside like a giant lantern". About one simile every 15 seconds.
- **Unnamed heroes.** Mostly "he", "the cashier", "the knight", "the blacksmith". Names appear only for comic or relatable characters (Gary, Jerry, Slow Eddie, Frank).
- **No intro, no outro, no "subscribe".** It starts on word one and ends on the twist.
- **Narrator persona:** "Rick" is a "regular guy who loves doing voiceovers" (channel bio). Warm, amused, slightly sarcastic storyteller, not a documentary voice.

## 4. Fill-in-the-blank template

About 210 to 240 words for a roughly 60 to 65 second Short.

```
[HOOK, 1 sentence, the strangest action, no context]
He/They [did baffling thing] [with one specific number/detail].

[CONTEXT, "You see,", 1 to 2 sentences]
You see, [world/stakes/competition in one breath].

[DOUBT, 2 to 3 sentences + 1 funny line of dialogue]
[Authority figure] [reacts badly]. "[Mocking one-liner]."
But he won't/doesn't [back down].

[RIVAL/CONTRAST, 2 sentences]
Meanwhile, [the "smart" approach] [wins at first]. Everyone [switches/laughs].

[ESCALATION, 3 time or number steps]
Then [time 1], [small change]. By [time 2], [bigger]. [Doubter gloats line].

[RE-HOOK]
Wait, [viewer's question]? Well, ...

[CLIMAX IMAGE, 1 to 2 sentences, show it before explaining it]
[The big moment arrives], and [the threat rises].

[REVEAL, "You see,", real mechanism + everyday analogy]
You see, [real fact]. Ever [everyday experience]? [Why it works in 1 sentence].

[STING, cut off mid-sentence or callback to the hook]
So [hero is fine], just as [rival/doubter gets the consequence, cut off...]
```

### Topic formula behind the hits
Pick a **real but little-known mechanism** (thermal expansion, a law loophole, a historic invention, a psychology effect), then **invent a small human drama** where someone gets mocked for using it before anyone understands it. Good areas from this channel: old engineering, sailing and pirates, the Wild West, gambling and lottery loopholes, Japanese workplace and law quirks, ancient inventions, prisons and psychology experiments.
