A busy street at dusk. Headlights. A bus. A girl on a bicycle, front wheel already over the curb. And then... everything stops. Raindrops hang in the air like glass beads. The bus driver’s mouth is open mid-shout. You can’t move. You’re inside the year again. But this time, a voice speaks inside your head. Don’t breathe out. Please. If you do... I don’t make it. Two weeks earlier, everything was finally calm. You’re Leo. Hold your breath, and one second becomes a whole year. You can think anything. You can’t move a finger. Last time, that year helped you save your grandpa’s life. He’s home now. Still dancing on Sundays. Just... a little slower. But something is changing. The power is growing. It used to wait for you. Now it doesn’t. A slammed door. A dropped plate. A car horn. Your breath catches... and you’re gone. Another year. You’re fifteen. But inside, you’ve now lived fifty-two years. So what happens when one second feels like a year... and you’re not the only one who knows? Then, in the middle of a frozen year, you hear something new. A voice. Hello? Is someone... actually there? You can’t turn your head. Neither can she. But somehow, you can think to each other. Her name is Nova. She’s sixteen. She lives three streets away. And she’s had the same power... for six years. How many years have you lived in here? I stopped counting at two hundred. Two hundred. You thought fifty was a lot. Plus: for the first time, the year isn’t silent. You talk about everything. Music. Books. Why cats knock things off tables. You play chess with no board, just in your heads. She wins. Every time. I’ve had practice, Leo. About a century of it. Minus: she knows where this is going. The years get longer, Leo. Mine used to be one. Now one second feels like two. Two years? Two years. In the real world, you finally meet on a park bench. Jake walks past. Dude. Who’s that? A friend. A really... old friend. But where it gets really strange... is the sky. In her years, Nova stares upward, so she counts stars. The light from some of those stars left them hundreds of years ago, she tells you. We’re both looking at the past. How many have you counted? Four thousand, one hundred and twelve. The price? Nova is tired. Really tired. Every time she breathes out, the real world feels too fast. Too loud. Too scary. So she stays in longer. And longer. Who could blame her? The year is quiet. Safe. And empty. People returning from long isolation often struggle to adjust, even to ordinary noise and crowds. Sometimes I think I should just... stay. Your old fear comes back. What if one day... you can’t breathe out? Now someone else is living it. And she’s starting to like it. So you bring her to Sunday dinner. Grandpa puts on his song. Boom. Boom. Boom. About a hundred beats a minute. Nova raises an eyebrow. You dance to this? It kept me alive, young lady. Then he gives her the line. A second is plenty, Nova... if you use it. You notice one thing about her that night. Before anything scary, Nova counts down. Three. Two. One. Before a test. Before a cold swimming pool. Before tasting Grandpa’s soup. Three... two... one. Nova, it’s soup. It’s very suspicious soup. Friday evening. Rain. You’re walking home. Across the street, Nova waits on her bike. The light changes. She pushes off. And a bus swings round the corner. Your breath catches. The year begins. And in it... her voice. Don’t breathe out. Please. If you do... I don’t make it. You study the frozen picture. The bus is about ten meters from her front wheel. At fifty kilometers per hour, it covers that in under a second. And it needs more than twenty meters to stop. Braking won’t save her. You’re three steps away. Your body still needs about a quarter of a second just to start moving. Then three steps. Then one grab. Can you make it? Maybe. Nova’s voice gets quiet. We could just stay. Here, nothing hits me. Here, you’re not alone either. A thousand years together... inside one second. And part of you wants it. No more empty years. What’s one second worth? Stay together forever... or step out together, now. Then you hear Grandpa’s beat in your head. Boom. Boom. Boom. A second is plenty... if you use it. Nova. We’ve had our years. Let’s use the second. Ready? Silence. Then... a whisper. Three. Two. One. You both breathe out. Same instant. The rain falls again. The horn screams. You take three steps. You grab her backpack. You pull. She brakes and leans back. The bus thunders past... close enough to lift her hair. Her front wheel stops right on the curb. The driver leans out. Are you two okay? You’re soaked. Shaking. Then laughing. Nova stares at you. Longest second of my life. And the shortest. On Sunday, Nova comes to dinner again. Grandpa’s song plays. And this time, she doesn’t count down. She just... dances. For the first time in two hundred years... neither of you is alone. That night, the power takes you again. And a voice speaks inside the year. Not Nova’s. Older. Colder. So. There are two of you now.
